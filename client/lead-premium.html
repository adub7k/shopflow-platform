Questions

Everything people ask before tinting

Is ceramic tint legal in New Mexico?
New Mexico allows tint on every window, with darkness limits that depend on which window it is. We know the current NM rules inside out and will recommend a setup that looks great and keeps you street-legal — just ask when you get your estimate.
What makes ceramic tint better than regular tint?
Regular dyed film mostly just darkens the glass — and it fades, turns purple, and bubbles within a few years. Ceramic film actually rejects heat and UV, doesn't interfere with phone/GPS signal, and keeps its color for the life of the vehicle. It costs more up front and it's worth it every summer after.
How long does installation take?
Most vehicles are done in a few hours — drop off in the morning, drive home the same day. We'll give you an exact time window when you book.
What does the lifetime warranty cover?
Fading, bubbling, peeling, and adhesive failure — for as long as you own the vehicle. If the film ever fails, we replace it. You deal with us directly, not a manufacturer's phone tree.
When can I roll my windows down?
Give the film 3–5 days to cure before rolling the tinted windows down. We'll send simple care instructions home with you so there's no guesswork.
I see small bubbles right after install — is that normal?
Yes. Tiny moisture pockets are a normal part of curing and disappear on their own within days (a bit longer in cold weather). Anything still visible after curing is covered by the warranty — we make it right.
How much does it cost?
It depends on your vehicle and how many windows you're tinting — that's exactly why the estimate is free and fast. Tell us what you drive and we'll text you a clear written price, usually within the hour during business hours.
Do I need to pay anything to get an estimate?
No. The estimate is completely free with no obligation. You only pay when you decide to book your install.
